# Multi-Intelligence Review

A workspace with one intelligence has one perspective. This pattern adds deliberate checkpoints where other intelligences scrutinize the work: specialist layers, other models, spawned agents built to argue. Each one carries a different character, different blind spots, and different instincts. Tapping the pool closes gaps that the primary conversation cannot see from inside it.

## The philosophy

The human and the primary agent think together, and that dialogue is productive but enclosed. It shares assumptions, shares context, and shares blind spots. At the right moments, the work gets swept by other intelligences: not to do the work, but to attack it, notch it up, and find what both thinkers missed.

This is not delegation. It is deliberately inviting disagreement.

## The two rhythms

**The checkpoint sweep.** During creative or planning sessions, the human streams ideas and the agent organizes and connects. At natural checkpoints, the specialist layer (or equivalent external intelligences) reviews what accumulated: scrutinize, judge, counter-argue, upgrade. The sweep happens after the thinking settles, not during it, because scrutiny too early kills the stream.

**The parallel panel.** When a plan or artifact is ready for judgment, spawn agents on different models with different mandates: one scrutinizes, one argues the counter-position, one explores what was not considered. Their findings come back to the primary conversation, which referees. Different characters produce different perceptions, and the gaps close before execution rather than during it.

## How to run it

1. **Reach a checkpoint, not perfection.** The work needs to be coherent enough to judge, not final.
2. **Pick the lens per checkpoint.** Match the intelligence to what the work is: architecture judgment for structure, adversarial review for robustness, a beginner's read for clarity.
3. **Give each reviewer the work and a mandate, not your conclusions.** Handing over your own reasoning biases the panel toward agreeing with you.
4. **Referee centrally.** Findings return to the primary conversation. The primary agent and the human decide what stands; reviewers do not get the last word.
5. **Record what changed and what was rejected.** Rejected findings are decisions too; the why keeps them from resurfacing every round.

## What this is not

- Not consensus-seeking. The goal is exposed gaps, not agreement.
- Not continuous. Between checkpoints, the primary dialogue runs undisturbed.
- Not unlimited. Each panel exists for one sweep, then dissolves.

## Why it works

Every intelligence has a character shaped by its training and its mandate. A panel of characters sees the work from angles no single conversation can hold, at a cost of minutes. The alternative is discovering the gaps in production, which is the most expensive review there is.
